The Thermoka Cooler

The Thermoka Solar-Thermal Absorption Clinic Cooler

An off-grid vaccine refrigerator that runs on stored solar heat — designed for the clinics the power grid doesn't reliably reach.

How it works

Heat in. Cold out. Stored for when the sun is down.

Instead of an electric compressor, Thermoka uses heat to drive its cooling — and it banks both heat and cold, so the vaccines stay protected overnight and through cloudy weather.

  1. 1

    Collect solar heat

    Solar thermal collectors capture heat directly from sunlight.

  2. 2

    Bank it in the hot store

    Heat is stored in a phase-change material, so the system has energy to draw on after sunset.

  3. 3

    Turn heat into cold

    A lithium-bromide absorption cycle uses the stored heat to produce cooling — with no mechanical compressor.

  4. 4

    Hold the vaccines at 2–8 °C

    A cold phase-change store keeps the vaccine chamber in range, buffering it against night-time and cloudy days.

Design features

Engineered for the last mile.

Absorption cooling

A lithium-bromide absorption cycle driven by solar heat replaces the electric compressor found in conventional fridges.

Hot thermal store

A phase-change heat store banks solar energy during the day so the cooling cycle can keep running after sunset.

Cold thermal store

A second phase-change store holds cold around the vaccine chamber, buffering it against gaps in solar supply.

Hardwired safety layer

PLC interlocks sit at the base of the control system, protecting the vaccines and the equipment independently of any software.

Predictive control

A supervisory controller reads weather forecasts and clinic usage patterns to decide when to prioritise cooling and when to recharge the stores.

Built to the standard

Designed toward WHO PQS E003, the performance standard for refrigerators used to store vaccines.

Specifications

Design targets

Thermoka 2.2 is the current design baseline. The figures here are targets for the prototype and will be refined through modelling and testing.

ApplicationVaccine and medicine storage in off-grid and rural clinics
Energy sourceSolar thermal — heat from sunlight
Cooling cycleAbsorption cycle, lithium bromide (no mechanical compressor)
Thermal storageDual phase-change stores — one hot, one cold
ControlHardwired PLC safety interlocks with a supervisory predictive controller
Storage temperature2–8 °C
CapacityApproximately 100 litres (target)
StandardDesigned toward WHO PQS E003

All figures are design targets and may change as development and testing progress.

Development roadmap

From design to deployment.

Thermoka is moving from paper to prototype, with funding secured through the Technology Innovation Agency's Grassroots Innovation Programme.

  1. Completed

    Thermoka 2.1

    First design revision of the solar-thermal absorption cooler.

  2. Current design

    Thermoka 2.2

    Revised design with a lithium-bromide cycle, dual hot and cold storage, and a tiered control system.

  3. Funded

    Working prototype

    Building the first working Thermoka, supported by TIA with Red Village Trading as implementing partner.

  4. Planned

    Testing toward WHO PQS E003

    Performance testing against the WHO standard for vaccine refrigerators.

  5. Goal

    Clinics across the Global South

    Reliable, grid-independent vaccine storage in off-grid and rural clinics — in South Africa and beyond.
