The Thermoka Cooler

Solar-thermal absorption clinic cooler

An off-grid vaccine refrigerator that runs on stored solar heat, designed for clinics the power grid doesn't reliably reach.

How it works

Heat in. Cold out. Stored for the night.

Instead of an electric compressor, Thermoka uses heat to drive its cooling. It stores both heat and cold, so vaccines stay protected overnight and through cloudy weather.

  1. 01

    Collect solar heat

    Solar thermal collectors capture heat directly from sunlight.

  2. 02

    Bank it in the hot store

    Heat is stored in a phase-change material, so there is energy to draw on after sunset.

  3. 03

    Turn heat into cold

    A lithium-bromide absorption cycle uses the stored heat to produce cooling, with no mechanical compressor.

  4. 04

    Hold vaccines at 2–8 °C

    A cold phase-change store keeps the vaccine chamber in range through the night and on cloudy days.

Overview

Design targets

Thermoka 2.2 is the current design baseline. These are targets for the prototype and will be refined through modelling and testing.

Application
Vaccine and medicine storage in off-grid and rural clinics
Energy source
Solar thermal: heat from sunlight
Cooling cycle
Absorption cycle, lithium bromide (no mechanical compressor)
Thermal storage
Dual phase-change stores: one hot, one cold
Control
Hardwired PLC safety interlocks with a supervisory predictive controller
Storage temperature
2–8 °C
Capacity
Approximately 100 litres (target)
Standard
Designed toward WHO PQS E003

All figures are design targets and may change as development and testing progress.

Design features

Engineered for the last mile

Absorption cooling

A lithium-bromide absorption cycle driven by solar heat replaces the electric compressor found in conventional fridges.

Hot thermal store

A phase-change heat store banks solar energy during the day so the cooling cycle can keep running after sunset.

Cold thermal store

A second phase-change store holds cold around the vaccine chamber, buffering it against gaps in solar supply.

Hardwired safety layer

PLC interlocks sit at the base of the control system, protecting vaccines and equipment independently of any software.

Predictive control

A supervisory controller reads weather forecasts and clinic usage patterns to decide when to cool and when to recharge the stores.

Built to the standard

Designed toward WHO PQS E003, the performance standard for refrigerators used to store vaccines.
