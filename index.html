The problem

The last mile is where the cold chain breaks.

Vaccines must stay between 2 °C and 8 °C from the factory to the patient. In off-grid and rural clinics, that chain often fails exactly where it matters most.

  • No reliable power

    Many rural clinics have no grid connection at all, or one that fails for hours at a time.

  • Temperature is unforgiving

    Vaccines exposed to too much heat, or to freezing, can lose their potency — often with no visible sign.

  • Communities miss out

    When stock spoils, immunisations are missed and scarce health budgets are wasted.

How it works

From sunlight to 2–8 °C, without the grid.

Thermoka stores the sun's energy as heat, then uses that heat to make cold — so it keeps working through the night and on cloudy days.

1

Collect

Solar thermal collectors capture heat from sunlight.

2

Store heat

Surplus heat is banked in a hot phase-change store for later use.

3

Make cold

A lithium-bromide absorption cycle turns that heat into cooling — no mechanical compressor.

4

Keep cold

A cold phase-change store holds the vaccine chamber at 2–8 °C, day and night.
